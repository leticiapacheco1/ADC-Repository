# MTQ Magnetic Field Response Test
This test measured the magnetic field response of the system using the onboard IMU magnetometer. The MTQ was initially kept OFF for 25 samples to establish a baseline Earth magnetic field reading, then energized for 75 samples at a fixed supply condition (3.3 V, 0.361 A), and finally switched OFF again for 25 samples to confirm return to baseline. The purpose of this test was to verify that the magnetorquer produces a measurable and repeatable perturbation in the local magnetic field. The system produced a peak magnetic field magnitude of approximately 28.3 µT under full actuation.

# Hanging Box Test
This test applied a constant magnetic dipole command of 0.4 A·m² to the magnetorquer without feedback control. The objective was to observe whether continuous dipole generation produces a measurable angular velocity change in the system when interacting with Earth’s magnetic field. Angular velocity about the Z-axis was recorded using the IMU gyro. This test isolates actuator performance under open-loop conditions and confirms if torque generation occurs as a result of dipole-field interaction rather than control feedback.

# Compass Needle Test
This experiment evaluated the spatial magnetic field produced by the MTQ using a compass and IMU-based magnetometer measurements. The MTQ was positioned at varying distances above the sensor, and the resulting magnetic field deviation was measured relative to a baseline Earth field. The difference in magnetic field magnitude was used to estimate dipole strength, and theoretical compass deflection angles were computed using the ratio of MTQ field to Earth’s horizontal field component. This test provided a direct visual and quantitative validation of magnetic field generation.

# Hardware Validation Insight 
Across all tests, a consistent magnetic field perturbation was observed when the MTQ was energized, confirming that the system successfully generates a measurable dipole moment under the given electrical input conditions. The measured field strength of approximately 28.3 µT under 3.3 V and 0.361 A operation validates the coil design (26 AWG, 7.5 cm winding length, 6 layers) as capable of producing functional magnetic actuation for attitude control applications. This current magnetorquer design will be present even better results once we improved the in-house manufacturing procedure using a mini lathe and a 3D printed tensioner.
